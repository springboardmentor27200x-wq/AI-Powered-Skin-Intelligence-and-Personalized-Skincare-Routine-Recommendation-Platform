1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Overview

In modern dermatology and consumer wellness, skincare routines often fail due to generic marketing, lack of chemical ingredient compatibility awareness, and the absence of objective skin monitoring. AI Skin Intelligence is a comprehensive digital platform that combines computer vision facial scanning, clinical weighted health scoring, chemical conflict detection, and personalized routine planning into a single accessible web ecosystem.

1.2 Objectives

  • Automated Visual Diagnostics: Implement camera-driven Computer Vision to detect dark spots, active blemishes/erythema, fine line edge density, and sebum reflectivity in real-time.
  • 5-Factor Weighted Health Scoring: Objectively evaluate overall skin health combining condition rating (35%), lifestyle (20%), sleep (15%), consistency (20%), and hydration (10%).
  • Chemical Incompatibility Detection: Prevent skin barrier damage by automatically analyzing formulations for conflicting active pairs (e.g. Retinol with Vitamin C or AHAs/BHAs).
  • Adaptive Routine Planning: Dynamically generate Morning, Evening, Weekly, and Seasonal regimens tailored to skin type, climate, UV index, and allergies.
  • Tele-Dermatology & Prescriptions: Connect consumers with certified clinical dermatologists for remote charting, treatment plans, and follow-up management.

1.3 Scope of the Project

Designed for Skincare Consumers, Dermatology Clinics, Wellness Consultants, and Healthcare Administrators, covering end-to-end skin diagnostic scanning, ingredient education, daily compliance tracking, and medical reports.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW

Traditionally, individuals rely on manual self-assessment, beauty influencer advice, or static cosmetic questionnaires. Key limitations include:

  • High Risk of Barrier Damage: Uninformed layering of conflicting active ingredients leads to chemical irritation and contact dermatitis.
  • Static & Non-Adaptive Plans: Existing apps fail to account for daily biological shifts (sleep debt, hydration levels) or environmental factors (UV index, regional climate).
  • Absence of Objective Visual Diagnostics: Lack of real-time computer vision to localize problem zones.
  • Disconnected Clinical Care: No integration exists between personal routine trackers and practicing dermatologists.

5.2 Computer Vision Feature Extraction Engine

# Computer Vision Feature Extraction (Snippet)
def analyze_facial_skin(image: Image.Image) -> dict:
    img = image.copy()
    img.thumbnail((600, 600), Image.Resampling.LANCZOS)
    rgb = np.array(img, dtype=np.float32)
    r, g, b = rgb[:, :, 0], rgb[:, :, 1], rgb[:, :, 2]
    
    # Skin Segmentation Mask
    skin_mask = (r > 50) & (g > 30) & (b > 15) & (r > g) & (g > b)
    total_skin = max(1, np.sum(skin_mask))

    # Dark Spot Localization (Gaussian Contrast Drop)
    gray = np.array(img.convert("L"), dtype=np.float32)
    blurred = np.array(img.convert("L").filter(ImageFilter.GaussianBlur(15)), dtype=np.float32)
    dark_spots = skin_mask & ((blurred - gray) > 18) & (gray < 170)
    dark_spot_sev = min(95.0, (np.sum(dark_spots) / total_skin) * 550.0)

    # Acne & Erythema Index (R - (G+B)/2)
    erythema = r - (g + b) / 2.0
    acne_mask = skin_mask & (erythema > 24.0) & (r > 100)
    acne_sev = min(95.0, (np.sum(acne_mask) / total_skin) * 600.0)

    overall_score = max(38.0, min(96.0, 100.0 - (dark_spot_sev * 0.28 + acne_sev * 0.32) * 0.65))
    return {"overall_skin_score": round(overall_score, 1), "dark_spots_sev": round(dark_spot_sev, 1), "acne_sev": round(acne_sev, 1)}
